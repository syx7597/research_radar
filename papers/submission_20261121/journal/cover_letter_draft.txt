To the Editors of Discover Computing,

Please consider "Dependency-consistent recovery supervision for executable knowledge question answering with small language models" as a Research article.

The manuscript studies how reference programs can supply executable continuation targets after a small language model has produced a divergent query history. The method maps reference dependencies to the actual intermediate-result handles, validates the suffix by deterministic replay, and trains on the continuation while retaining the observed divergence as context.

The empirical study uses Qwen2.5-3B-Instruct with paired ordinary and recovery continuation training. On 2,000 project-held-out KQA Pro questions, the average accuracy across two continuation seeds rises from 78.75% to 83.50%, with a question-paired 95% interval of [3.53, 5.98] percentage points for the gain. A separate construction diagnostic examines dependency consistency in 414 comparable historical training pairs. The paper provides a controlled study of the complete training scheme, its execution behavior, and its inference cost.

The work addresses natural language processing, machine learning, and executable information access within the journal's scope. The methodological contribution is the use of explicit reference-program structure to construct and verify continuation targets for actual histories. All reported model outcomes come from completed experiments; identical-history continuation evaluation is discussed as future work.
